## 1. Crypto hedge fund performance by strategy type (incl. 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026 YTD); did systematic/trend beat BTC risk-adjusted?

### Takeaway
Live fund-index data (Galaxy VisionTrack, Crypto Insights Group, Eurekahedge) shows quant/systematic directional funds lost less than fundamental/discretionary funds in 2022 (-26.6% vs -57.4%) and did best in the 2024 bull market (+53.7%), but still badly lagged BTC's ~+120% in 2024. Directional funds (including quant directional) lost money in 2025 (-10.1%) and again in 2026 YTD (quant directional -6.4%). The only consistently positive strategy in live data is market neutral (+18.5% in 2024, +14.1% in 2025, ~19% annualised over three years with low drawdowns), and that is mostly basis/funding/arbitrage, which is not available to small US spot-only retail accounts.

### Inferences
- Live evidence that systematic crypto funds beat buy-and-hold BTC on raw returns is weak. That is a risk-adjusted argument, not a return argument.
- The persistent live winner is market-neutral (basis, funding and arbitrage capture). It needs derivatives or shorting, which Alpaca and Coinbase spot accounts do not offer, so it does not transfer to the target retail setup.
- The pattern fits trend-following theory: it pays in sustained trends (2024 rally, early-2026 selloff) and bleeds in choppy markets (2025).

## 3. Retail bot, copy-trading and retail-trader profitability outcomes

### Takeaway
Most retail crypto participants lose money. The BIS found a majority of crypto-app users in nearly all of 95 economies lost money on BTC (2015-2022). Copy-trading data is mostly vendor-sourced but consistently poor: about 48% of followers profitable in one 100k-account study, and fewer than 7% of 292k wallets profitable on a social trading app. Lead traders profit far more often than their followers. No rigorous independent study of 3Commas or Pionex bot user outcomes exists; published bot results suffer heavy survivorship bias. Non-crypto day-trading studies show that fewer than 1-3% of persistent day traders profit.

### Cited Findings
- BIS Bulletin No 69, "Crypto shocks and retail losses" (Cornelli, Doerr, Frost, Gambacorta, 20 Feb 2023): a majority of crypto app users in nearly all economies lost money on their bitcoin holdings. Data covers Aug 2015 to Dec 2022 across 95 countries plus on-chain data. Users bought as prices rose, and the big holders ("whales") sold to them — [BIS Bulletin 69](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.htm); [BIS PDF](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.pdf)
- BIS median-investor simulation (assumes buying on app-download day plus $100/month): median loss $431 of $900 invested by Dec 2022 — [Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.com/retail-crypto-investors-emerging-economies-162932915.html); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/213372/majority-of-bitcoin-retail-investors-lost-money-in-the-last-seven-years-bis). A secondary claim that "73-81% lost money" could not be verified against the bulletin — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/121763/bitcoin-after-2015-lost-money-bis-report)
- Copy trading (VENDOR SOURCE, competing exchange KuCoin; study authors not named): 90-day study of >100k copy-trading outcomes on Binance, Bybit and MEXC. 48.48% of followers were profitable overall. Platform figures: Binance 66.50%, Bybit 43.65%, MEXC 57.79%. 97% of lead traders had positive personal PnL, but only 43.61% delivered positive returns to followers — [KuCoin blog, "Is crypto copytrading profitable in 2026?"](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/ua-is-crypto-copytrading-profitable-in-2026)
- Fomo social/copy-trading app (DWF Ventures research): fewer than 7% of 292,000 wallets made a profit over three months. Callers profit when followers' buying pushes the price up, and followers absorb the drop when callers sell — [Yellow.com](https://yellow.com/news/fomo-copy-trading-94-percent-wallet-losses)
- Academic: Carnegie Mellon (Kawai et al., CHI 2024) studies crypto-derivatives copy trading. Platforms advertise "most profitable" and "most liked" portfolios on landing pages, which drives selection bias — [Kawai et al., CHI'24 PDF](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf)
- Bond University study of 25 million trades on a social trading platform: copycat investors take higher-risk trades that end up losing money — [Bond University](https://bond.edu.au/news/fomo-wrecking-your-portfolio)
- eToro CFD disclosure: 50-61% of retail CFD accounts lose money, depending on entity and date (eToro page says 50%; third parties cite 51%, 52% as of 6 May 2026, 55%, 61%). No crypto-specific figure. Industry average about 71% across 49 brokers — [eToro press page](https://www.etoro.com/news-and-analysis/press-releases/retail-investors-come-of-age-displaying-discipline-and-diversification/); [BrokerRank retail loss rates](https://brokerrank.net/research/retail-loss-rates); [ForexBrokers eToro](https://forexbrokers.com/reviews/etoro)
- Day-trading benchmarks (non-crypto): Taiwan, 450,000 traders over 15 years, fewer than 1% earned reliable positive returns net of fees, and more than 80% lost money in any six-month period. Brazil futures (Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti): about 3% of roughly 1,600 persistent day traders made money over a year — [Financemagnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/analysis/new-study-shows-just-how-unprofitable-day-trading-is/amp); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/retail-day-trading-is-an-uphill-battle/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/11/20/attention-robinhood-power-users-most-day-traders-lose-money.html)
- Bots (3Commas/Pionex): no rigorous user-outcome study found. Altrady (itself a bot vendor) notes that selection bias dominates the public record because losing users quietly switch bots off. Grid bots profit in sideways markets and "bleed capital in a strong trend" — [Altrady blog](https://www.altrady.com/blog/crypto-bots/are-ai-crypto-trading-bots-profitable-2026). Affiliate review sites report conflicting returns (+84.2% over six months vs +14.2% over 90 days with manual re-ranging every two weeks); treat as marketing — [Earnifyhub](https://earnifyhub.com/crypto-web3/best-crypto-trading-bots-comparison-2026). One review says 3Commas' backtester overstates profits because it assumes immediate fills; Pionex has no backtester — [bestcryptobots](https://bestcryptobots.netlify.app/2025/01/3commas-vs-pionex-which-crypto-bot-actually-makes-money-2025-comparison)

### Inferences
- In copy trading, leader profitability does not transfer to followers: 97% of leaders profitable vs about 44% delivering profits to followers in the vendor data. Slippage, entry lag, and leaders trading against follower flow are the likely mechanisms.
- For retail, the base rate is that most active traders lose. A strategy that only matches buy-and-hold after costs is already above median retail outcomes.

- No SEC/FINRA study specific to retail crypto trader profitability found.

## 4. Win rate vs profitability among professional systematic/trend traders

### Takeaway
Trend-following systems in crypto typically win only about 28-45% of trades. They profit through payoff asymmetry: average winners several times larger than average losers, with a few big trends paying for many small losses. Theory confirms that a low win fraction is compatible with positive expectancy. Targeting high win rates usually means cutting winners short, which removes the right-tail payoff trend strategies depend on.

### Cited Findings
- Theory: trend followers "lose more often than they gain". You can make money on average even with a low winning fraction f, provided the average gain per winner exceeds the average loss per loser. A 40% win fraction implies winners are held roughly twice as long as losers — [Potters & Bouchaud, arXiv physics/0508104](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0508104)
- Crypto backtest, 5 years, 4,791 trades (VENDOR: altFINS): trend following won only 28% of trades. Average winner +44.7%, average loser -9.7%; a few large trends paid for all the small losses — [altFINS](https://altfins.com/knowledge-base/which-trading-strategy-works-crypto-stocks-forex-backtest)
- BTC moving-average study (individual research): win rate about 30% overall, ranging from about 39% for short holding periods to about 17% for the slowest configuration; profitable because of large reward-to-risk asymmetry — [notp1ssed trend-following BTC study](https://notp1ssed.github.io/portfolio/assets/docs/002_TrendFollowing_Strategy_bitcoin.html)
- BTC H4 trend system (MQL5 blog): 48.6% win rate over 146 trades; 44.4% in 2022 — [MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/770303)
- Vendor blog rule of thumb: trend following has a 25-50% win rate — [HyroTrader](https://www.hyrotrader.com/bg/blog/most-profitable-trading-strategy/)
- Man AHL: a single crypto market's Sharpe is positive but small, and diversification across 10-15 coins is the key to risk-adjusted returns — [Man Institute](https://www.man.com/maninstitute/in-crypto-we-trend)

### Inferences
- A "high win rate" target conflicts with how live profitable systematic approaches work. Strategies with high hit rates (grid bots, mean reversion, tight take-profits) carry negative skew: many small wins and occasional large losses in trends, which matches the grid-bot "bleeds in a strong trend" observation in section 3.

### Gaps
- Direct quotes from Andreas Clenow (Following the Trend / Stocks on the Move) and Michael Covel (Trend Following) on win rates were not retrieved in this session. Their commonly cited view that trend followers win about 30-40% of trades is not verified here.
- No crypto quant fund interviews with disclosed live win-rate statistics found.

## 5. Practical constraints for US retail: Alpaca Crypto, Coinbase Advanced, (Robinhood); US tax / wash sale

On small accounts, fees matter a lot. Alpaca's tier 1 is 0.15% maker / 0.25% taker. Coinbase Advanced's entry tier is 0.40% maker / 0.60% taker. A round trip therefore costs about 0.3-0.5% (Alpaca) or 0.8-1.2% (Coinbase) before spread, which rules out high-frequency or small-edge strategies and favours low-turnover trend/rotation systems. Both are spot-only for this purpose: no shorting and no margin in Alpaca crypto (not confirmed in docs this session). Market-neutral strategies are therefore unavailable. As of Nov 2026, the US wash-sale rule still does not apply to directly held crypto, but several bills would extend it.
